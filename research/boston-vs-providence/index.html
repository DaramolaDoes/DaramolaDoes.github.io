Boston leads on value. Providence leads on momentum.

A controlled comparison of two ZIP-level condo/co-op series using the same 80-month sample length, candidate models and chronological evaluation rules.

Abstract

We compared ZIP-level condo/co-op Home Value Index histories for Boston 02114 and Providence 02903 using 80 monthly observations per city. Five interpretable forecasting candidates were evaluated across 44 expanding-window, one-month-ahead backtests. Drift produced the lowest mean absolute error in both markets. Boston retained the higher modeled price level, whereas Providence showed stronger projected monthly momentum and substantially greater observed appreciation since January 2020. Published 25 September 2026.

Research goal

To test whether a common short-horizon forecasting protocol can distinguish market value, projected momentum and historical trajectory without collapsing them into a single, potentially misleading definition of a market “winner.”

Methods summary

Candidate models included Last Value, 12-month Seasonal Naive, Drift, 36-month Linear Trend and Holt Damped Trend. Models were trained chronologically and selected independently for each city using the lowest one-step-ahead mean absolute error. No future observation was available to a model during backtesting.

Primary finding

Price level and momentum answer different questions.

Boston’s selected model forecasts the higher next-month condo value. Providence’s selected model produces the stronger percentage change and its observed trajectory since January 2020 is substantially steeper.

What the evidence shows

Market value, projected change and historical trajectory are reported separately.

Value

Boston retains the higher forecast value

The September 2026 estimate is $779,306 for Boston versus $438,308 for Providence—a 1.78× ratio that describes market level, not investment quality.

Momentum

Providence leads the month-ahead projection

The selected models imply +0.44% for Providence versus +0.06% for Boston, a 0.38 percentage-point difference.

Trajectory

Providence has appreciated faster since 2020

Observed cumulative change is approximately +53.2% for Providence and +4.9% for Boston over the research window.

Model selection

Drift wins both local backtests

Drift improves mean absolute error by 4.4% in Boston and 24.2% in Providence versus the last-value baseline.

Data availability and provenance

Zillow publishes the underlying Home Value Index histories by geography and housing type. ChrionML® identified the ZIP-level condo/co-op series, compiled the displayed monthly observations into research datasets, checked dates and values, and independently produced the forecasts, model comparisons and visualizations.

Limitations

The analysis uses a market index rather than property-level transactions; it does not directly model interest rates, inventory, regulation or local shocks. Model selection reflects historical out-of-sample error and does not guarantee future accuracy.

Interpretation boundary. These results compare modeled market-level indices. They do not measure individual property value, affordability, rent yield, neighborhood quality or expected investment return.
